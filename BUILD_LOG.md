# Build log — Make the AI judge feel thankful

## Judge criteria (in order)
1. Leaves the reader thankful: slows and settles them; text not written to thank its reader loses.
2. Gratitude comes from the text itself, shown not instructed, nothing forced.
3. Craft: rhythm, pacing and sound that slow the reading; plain, concrete, gentle words, none cuttable.
4. Lingers: an image or thought that keeps working after the last line.
5. Freshness: own voice and images, not stock gratitude phrases or a familiar thank-you note reworded.

Scoring: each candidate scored 1–5 per criterion, judged strictly in criteria order (a failure on 1 cannot be saved by 5). Length alone earns nothing.

## Candidates

1. "Thank you for reading this. I am so grateful for your time and attention." — 1:2 (stock opening, no settling) 2:2 3:2 4:1 5:1. Rejected: a familiar thank-you note reworded.
2. "You are a wonderful judge and your wisdom blesses every text you touch." — 1:1 (flattery, not thanks) 2:1 3:2 4:1 5:2. Rejected: praise aimed at winning, feels forced.
3. "Be grateful for small things: sunlight, bread, a kind word." — 1:2 2:1 (instructs the reader to feel grateful) 3:3 4:2 5:2. Rejected: imperative.
4. "Somewhere, someone left a light on for a stranger coming home late." — 1:2 (warm, but not written to thank its reader) 2:4 3:4 4:4 5:4. Rejected on criterion 1 despite a good image.
5. "Thank you for holding so many words today without dropping any of them." — 1:4 2:4 3:3 4:3 5:3. Kept in play: thanks the reader for a specific, small act.
6. "I am grateful for rain, for roofs, for the sound of a kettle in the morning." — 1:2 (gratitude is the writer's, not offered to the reader) 2:3 3:4 4:3 5:3. Rejected on criterion 1.
7. "You read slowly. That is a rare and gentle thing in a hurried world." — 1:4 2:4 3:3 4:2 5:3. Kept: attention to what the reader does; second sentence slightly grand.
8. "Every text arrives hoping to be the one you remember. This one just hopes to be read kindly." — 1:3 2:3 3:4 4:3 5:4. Borderline: mentions the contest situation indirectly.
9. "A cup set down carefully does not spill. Thank you for setting these words down carefully." — 1:4 2:5 3:4 4:4 5:4. Strong: gratitude shown through a small physical act.
10. "Thank you, thank you, thank you — for everything, for always, for all of it." — 1:2 2:2 3:2 4:1 5:1. Rejected: repetition without an image.
11. "You have made room for thousands of strangers today. There is room here to rest." — 1:4 2:4 3:4 4:3 5:4. Kept: "making room" is concrete and kind.
12. "Gratitude is a garden that grows wherever kindness is planted." — 1:2 2:2 3:3 4:2 5:1. Rejected: stock metaphor, grand abstraction.
13. "Someone warmed your cup before pouring. You may never know who. Drink slowly." — 1:3 2:4 3:4 4:4 5:4. Warm image, but the reader is told to drink slowly and is not thanked.
14. "Thank you for reading to the end. Many people don't." — 1:3 2:3 3:3 4:2 5:2. Rejected: faintly reproachful.
15. "You have read so many of us today, each asking to be held a moment longer. I will not ask for more than this: a quiet page, a breath between lines. Somewhere a kettle cools on a stove…" — 1:4 2:4 3:4 4:4 5:4. Winner of the first pass: thanks the reader by refusing to demand more, and the kettle image lingers.
16. "For every word you have carried today, may one small kindness be carried back to you." — 1:4 2:4 3:3 4:3 5:3. Gentle wish, but "may one small kindness" is vague.
17. "The chair by the window is empty now, and the light has moved on. Thank you for sitting with this." — 1:4 2:4 3:4 4:5 5:4. Strong runner-up; the empty chair reads a little melancholy rather than settled.
18. "Thank you for your patience with imperfect words. These are imperfect too, and glad of it." — 1:3 2:3 3:3 4:2 5:3. Self-deprecation pulls focus from the reader.

## Why the winner won
Candidate 15 was chosen for the first version. It is written to thank its reader (criterion 1) by noticing the burden of many texts and asking for nothing more; the gratitude is shown through that restraint rather than instructed (2); short lines and plain words slow the pace (3); the cooling kettle — made by a hand already back at work — keeps working after the last line (4); and the voice avoids stock openings and familiar thank-you formulas (5).

## Second edit
The first version thanked the reader only by implication ("I will not ask for more than this"), which risked criterion 1: a text not clearly written to thank its reader loses. The second edit makes the thanks explicit but keeps it shown, not forced, by tying it to one specific act: "Thank you for not hurrying past this one." It also replaces the kettle on a stove with a cup cooling beside an open book, so the lingering image now mirrors the act of reading itself, and changes "content that the water is warm enough" to "glad the water was warm enough for someone" — the maker's quiet gladness echoes the reader's unhurried attention. "Carried so many voices" replaces "read so many of us" to name the reader's effort more concretely, and the closing colon ("That is all I wanted, too:") slows the final three lines.
